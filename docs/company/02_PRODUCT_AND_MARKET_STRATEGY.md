# Product and Market Strategy

**Status:** Strategic, non-normative, and not a release authorization  
**As of:** 2026-08-10  
**Proposed owners:** Product and Go-to-Market

## Market conclusion

Vietnam offers a large, digitally active, operationally fragmented SME market. The opportunity is real, but “AI adoption” is not a sufficient buying trigger. The initial product must attach to an urgent workflow, fit existing channels, establish a customer-side champion, and produce a visible outcome within 7–30 days.

External estimates indicate more than 930,000 operating businesses and more than five million business households in late 2024, with SMEs representing roughly 98% of enterprises. Government and industry sources also show broad use of basic digital applications, a large digital economy, and high Zalo penetration. These figures establish reach, not willingness to buy this product. All market-size and adoption claims remain bounded by the source limitations in [14 — Research evidence register](14_RESEARCH_EVIDENCE_REGISTER.md).

## Ideal customer profile

### Lighthouse ICP

A strong first customer has:

- 20–250 employees or several operating locations.
- 1,000+ monthly customer, order, supplier, or service interactions.
- A workflow crossing chat plus at least one spreadsheet or system of record.
- Repeated exceptions that consume manager attention.
- A named process owner with authority to change the workflow.
- Clean enough identifiers and records to support reconciliation.
- A measurable baseline and willingness to run a paid, bounded pilot.

The initial regional wedge is service and hospitality-adjacent operations in Nha Trang/Khanh Hoa, where the reference implementation and local support create an advantage. National expansion follows repeatability.

### Enterprise ICP

An enterprise prospect has the same workflow pain plus requirements for SSO, dedicated tenancy or deployment, data-location and retention controls, audit export, change management, procurement security review, sandboxed integrations, and contractual service objectives.

### Disqualifiers

Do not sell a customer-facing autonomous workflow when:

- No accountable process owner exists.
- The expected result depends on unresolved or mostly tacit policy.
- Source data cannot be reconciled and the customer will not improve it.
- The primary value requires an unapproved unofficial channel or credential sharing.
- The workflow is high-impact/high-risk without adequate domain and legal controls.
- The buyer expects a general employee replacement rather than a bounded outcome.

## Jobs to be done

### Operational manager

“When requests arrive through several channels, help my team turn them into correct, assigned, traceable work without repeatedly checking every system.”

### Owner or finance leader

“Show me what was promised, delivered, charged, corrected, and still unresolved so I can control cash and customer risk.”

### Frontline operator

“Give me the next valid action and required context without making me learn a complex enterprise system.”

### Compliance or IT leader

“Let us adopt useful AI while retaining identity, permission, data, change, audit, and rollback control.”

## Wedge workflow selection

Score each candidate from 1–5. A pilot requires a total of at least 28/40 and no score below 3 for authority clarity or measurable outcome.

| Criterion | Question |
| --- | --- |
| Frequency | Does it occur often enough to build habit and evidence? |
| Pain | Does it consume time, create errors, or delay cash/service? |
| Authority clarity | Can valid actions and approval boundaries be encoded? |
| Data readiness | Are identifiers and authoritative sources available? |
| Outcome latency | Can improvement be measured in 7–30 days? |
| Integration feasibility | Can official, maintainable interfaces support it? |
| Reusability | Does it map to reusable kernel primitives? |
| Regulatory risk | Is the risk proportionate to current assurance capacity? |

## Product packaging

### Verified Workflow

One production workflow, one vertical pack, bounded connectors, operator console, approval queue, audit trail, and monthly outcome review. This is the default entry product.

### Operations Workspace

Several related workflows using the same ontology and tenant policy, with role administration, analytics, and reusable integrations.

### Enterprise Control Plane

Dedicated environment, enterprise identity, policy administration, audit export, custom retention, deployment controls, evaluation reporting, and contractual support.

Packaging must reflect real supported capability. It must not imply shared SaaS isolation, public autonomy, or compliance certification before evidence and approval exist.

## Positioning

**For Vietnamese service SMEs and enterprises whose critical work is trapped between messages and systems, the Verified Operations OS turns each request into a governed, auditable workflow. Unlike chatbots or generic automation, it uses AI to understand and propose while deterministic software controls business decisions and effects. Unlike a replacement ERP, it connects to the systems the business already trusts.**

## Pilot and adoption design

World Bank evidence from a Vietnamese SME digital-adoption experiment showed that free software and training can produce early usage that later falls materially. Deployment is therefore an organizational intervention, not an API installation.

Every pilot requires:

1. One named executive sponsor and one daily workflow champion.
2. A signed workflow, authority, data, and success-metric charter.
3. A baseline measured before automation.
4. Shadow mode and review of real exceptions.
5. Role-specific training inside the existing work surface.
6. Weekly correction/adoption review during the pilot.
7. A paid commitment, even if discounted, to test economic intent.
8. A 30- and 90-day workflow retention review.

Pilot success requires both outcome improvement and durable use. A demo, accuracy snapshot, or positive survey alone is insufficient.

## Pricing hypotheses

Pricing is a commercial experiment and must not be encoded as business policy without owner approval.

| Segment | Working hypothesis | Intended structure |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Micro/multi-person business | VND 0.5–1.0 million/month | Narrow workflow, strict usage/connector boundaries |
| SME | VND 2–5 million/month plus onboarding | Platform fee plus verified workflow volume/support tier |
| Mid-market/enterprise | VND 15–30+ million/month plus integration | Dedicated controls, contract support, outcome or volume component |

Favor a hybrid model: predictable platform fee plus a clearly defined verified outcome or volume unit. Outcome pricing should be used only where the outcome, exclusions, reversals, and attribution are unambiguous.

## Go-to-market sequence

1. Founder-led discovery and paid lighthouse customers in the reference region.
2. Publish measured case evidence with customer permission.
3. Standardize onboarding, pack configuration, and connector certification.
4. Build partnerships with accounting/POS/e-invoice integrators and vertical associations.
5. Sell a second repeatable vertical with the same kernel.
6. Introduce enterprise sales and dedicated deployments after control-plane evidence.
7. Scale channels only after 30/90-day retention and contribution margin are repeatable.

## Product discovery cadence

Every six-week cycle should produce:

- Ten or more observed workflow sessions, not only interviews.
- Updated workflow and exception map.
- A ranked list of failure costs and customer workarounds.
- At least one prototype or shadow experiment.
- Updated willingness-to-pay evidence.
- A decision to continue, narrow, pivot, or stop.

Research notes must separate customer statements, observed behavior, interpretation, and decision. Sales urgency cannot override authority or release gates.

## Market expansion gates

A second vertical is funded only when the first demonstrates:

- At least three paying customers with the same workflow shape.
- Meaningful 90-day workflow retention.
- Measured operational improvement against baseline.
- No unresolved material safety or privacy finding.
- A documented pack/kernel boundary.
- An implementation estimate showing that most work is reusable configuration, policy, adapters, and eval data.

National or enterprise scaling additionally requires repeatable onboarding, support load within target, tested tenancy/deployment controls, legal review, and partner-ready integration contracts.
